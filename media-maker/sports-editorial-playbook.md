# Media Maker — US sports editorial playbook
Version: 2026-10-11. Editorial examples and operating rules; not a model fine-tune or automated sports feed.

## Segment lanes
- Comeback: state the actual deficit, when it existed, turning point and final result. Distinguish game comeback from series comeback.
- Unexpected ending: describe decisive verified play without inventing crowd reaction or player intent.
- Standout player: give relevant verified statistics and context. Never call a performance a record without specifying scope and checking the original record.
- Local flavor: select city, teams, colleges and geographic radius. Local fan edition may change story order and phrasing, never the score or facts.
- Community and participation: women's sports, high school, youth, disability sports and local clubs. Do not invent local results when sources are missing.
- What to watch next: schedules are distinct from final results; confirm location and time zone.

## Required event record
Sport, league, teams, event date, venue, final/in-progress/postponed status, score, timestamp and zone, official result URL, box score URL, decisive play, player-stat evidence, local audience relevance, highlight rights.

## Release rules
Final results need primary league/team/competition evidence or an explicit unresolved-source hold. Cross-check conflicting reports against the original box score. If unresolved, omit the disputed detail and retain a HOLD for affected claim.
Do not report live games as final. Do not promote scheduled games as completed. Refresh before airing.
Keep standings and rankings tied to their effective date.
Use upbeat pacing for sport; injury, death and abuse stories require measured delivery.
Highlight links do not grant redistribution rights. Use licensed footage or clearly labeled original graphics.
Save segment time, audience profile and pronunciation notes. Exact voice selection is independent of geography.
Human release approval remains required.
